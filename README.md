# The-Virtual-Eye

The virtual eye is a multidisciplinary initiative to develop a simulation of the eye in health and disease. The virtual eye has three aims:
1. To enhance our understanding of the eye
2. Provide an environment to screen potential treatments for eye disease 
3. Enable personalised treatment regimes utilising digital twin approaches 
 
The collaborators span a range of disciplines, including but not limited to, ophthalmology, cellular biology, mathematics, and engineering. The initiative is supported by researchers and Investigators from the UK, USA and Europe.

A digital twin is an accurate, dynamic digital replica of a physical patient or organ within that patient. These replicas can be used for personalized care, for example by modeling the natural history of a disease, making predictions on a patient's prognosis or simulating the effect of different treatment strategies. Bidirectional data flows enable representation of a physical system in a virtual space and provide a return prediction or recommendation to intervene in the real-world setting. Glucose monitors and insulin delivery are a useful example in current medical practice. Other uses could involve the simulation of synthetic clinical trials, for example when a comparison with an untreated group would be unethical, or the discovery of novel therapeutic targets and disease mechanisms.

The growing excitement around digital twins in healthcare is fostered by recent developments genetic sequencing, deep phenotyping, mechanistic modeling and artificial intelligence. However, a digital twin is generally not any of these elements in isolation, but rather a cohesive effort to bridge these technologies to produce accurate patient's replicas (patient-in-silico) and a dynamic synchronization with the physical patient. Such an approach is particularly attractive to parse and synthesize the ever-increasing volume of clinical information and complex health data, transforming healthcare from a collection of broadly applied guidelines to a truly personalized process.

Translational Vision Science and Technology invites submissions for a special issue on Digital Twins and Personalized Medicine in Eye Care. The special issue welcomes papers presenting original research in translational eye care and vision science using experimental data, real-world and clinical trial cohorts, simulations and advanced modeling.

Topics include, but are not limited to:

Development and application of mechanistic and predictive models with a clear potential of becoming components of a digital twin technology
Integration of individual systems for the development of multi-scale digital twin models
Applications of digital twins for clinical trial design/simulation and the discovery of novel disease mechanisms and therapeutic targets
